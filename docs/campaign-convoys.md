# Protected campaign convoys

The authored chronicle now has a physical convoy contract rather than treating a cargo delivery plus kills as equivalent to protecting ships. `scripts/generate-campaign-convoys.mjs` compiles the pinned native `save` groups with `accompany` or escort personality (excluding derelicts) into `src/campaign-convoys.js`; it does not load the full source data in the browser.

The compiled checkpoint contains **60 stages and 196 member records**, with a largest single group of **14 ships**. This includes the six named freighters from `FW Escort 1`, prisoner transports, Dreadnought/Fury formation, medical and nuclear convoys, Raven's Eye of Horus, and the post-Pug N.S. Peacemaker. Later faction, research, refugee, commercial and diplomatic groups keep their source models and names. Unnamed fleet members get display labels; where a source fleet has variants the compiler records the deterministic first variant. Repeated appearances of the same named ship inside a condensed stage are deduplicated rather than cloned.

`campaignMethods(Game)` installs these methods:

- `campaignConvoyAcceptance(mission)` returns `{ok,message}`; at most one protected convoy is active. Ordinary cargo/passenger work remains concurrent. An existing native protected mission also occupies the slot; the native bridge must enforce the reverse exclusion.
- `syncCampaignActors()` merges records with `scope: 'campaign'` into `state.missionActors`, preserving native records. Each actor has its source hull, name and faction, a persistent member-health record and a visit-specific ID.
- `escortStatus(active)`, `escortObjective(active)` and `escortObjectiveReady(active)` report the alive/arrived count and gate completion.
- `campaignDepartureReady()` rejects jumping away from a surviving disabled companion. `campaignLandingReady()` prevents leaving the arrival evidence incomplete at the destination. A failed convoy can always land for recovery.
- `campaignActorEvent(event)` accepts renderer-scoped damage, destruction, disability, assistance, confirmed capture, hail and safe-arrival events. Unrelated or stale-visit events cannot fulfill the objective. Disabled ships remain alive and must be physically assisted before they can follow or arrive. Further damage can destroy them. Confirmed capture or destruction fails protection; disability alone does not.
- `retryEscort({missionId,arcId})` is available in port after a loss. It replaces the convoy, resets the existing assignment's flight objectives, and charges a fee. Any shortfall becomes loan debt, preserving a recovery path. It does not duplicate mission cargo or advance the story.

Safe arrival requires the renderer's physical escort check: active and alive, near the dock and flagship, with no active hostile contacts. The model rechecks hostility. A safe campaign ship gets `departed` for that visit, representing a completed docking and releasing its physics slot. This permits fleets larger than an individual presentation wave to finish rather than permanently starving queued ships. Hull health persists into the next system; arrival proofs do not.

Integration hooks: check acceptance before creating a protected assignment; sync after acceptance, travel, launch, landing, completion, rescue and save restore; include escort readiness in mission completion; route `sourceActorEvent` for campaign IDs before the native-engine guard; expose port retry. The renderer already supports `departed` removal and actor-identity events. Final integrated browser behavior remains subject to the root verification pass.

The hooks are exercised by the actual Game in `tests/expanded-arcs.test.js`: the earned main/baseline checkpoint and all expanded continuations use named actor arrival receipts, reload between stages and retain source roster counts. Dedicated integration cases prove disabled survivors can be assisted after save/load, destroyed convoys can land and retry without duplicating cargo, a second protected assignment is rejected, and a 14-member formation cannot land after only its first 12 arrivals. These are explicit modeled renderer receipts; physical positioning, targeting and rescue interaction require the separate browser evidence in `docs/verification.md`.

`node --test tests/campaign-actors.test.js` passes thirteen focused tests: native roster/armament provenance, disability/assistance/capture, arrival gating, travel/save persistence, failure/recovery, retry economics without cargo duplication, presentation-wave release, coexistence/cleanup, hostile checks and concurrency.
